## Linear Momentum:

Defined as a product of **Mass** and **Velocity** and is represented as $\vec{p}$  and is defined as 
$$
\vec{p} = m*\vec{v}
$$

`m` is the mass of the object and $\vec{v}$ is the velocity of the object. 

Direction of momentum is along the direction of  $\vec{v}$. 

SI Units: $kg.m/s$

Momentum of an object depends on its $\text{Mass}$ and $\text{Velocity}$ 

So greater the momentum  of an object higher the harder its to stop, or the object has greater effect on
another object when they interact. 

- Force is required to change the momentum of an object, whether to increase the momentum, to decrease it,
  or to change direction. 

- The original definition of newton's second law was given in momentum. (called it as $\text{m.v}$ ). 

- So Newtons second law can stated as:

> The rate of change of momentum of an object equals to the net force applied on it. 

$$
 \sum{\vec{F}} = \frac{\Delta \vec{p}}{\Delta t}     \vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \frac{\Delta \vec{p}}{\Delta t}
$$

- $\sum{\vec{F}}$ : Net force applied on the object ( Vector sum of all the forces acting on it. )
- $\Delta \vec{v}$ : Resulting momentum change that occurs during the time interval $\Delta t$.


Newtons second law can be written as:

For object of mass $\text{m}$

 - $\vec{v_i}$ is initial velocity. 
 - $\vec{v_f}$ is final velocity. 
 
then 

$\sum{\vec{F}} = \frac{\Delta \vec{p}}{\Delta t} = \frac{ m\vec{v_f} - m\vec{v_i}}{\Delta t} = \frac{m \Delta {\vec{v}}}{\Delta t}$

This can be written as $\sum{\vec{F}} = m \vec{a}$, since $\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \vec{a}$

## Conservation of Momentum:

In an elastic collision of two objects with initial momentum 

$\vec{p_{1i}} = m_1 . \vec{v_{1i}}$ and $\vec{p_{2i}} = m_2 . \vec{v_{2i}}$ 

After the collision there momentum changes, the sum of there momentum before is same as the sum momentum
after the collision ( elastic collision has no loss of energy )

$\vec{p_{1f}} = m_1 . \vec{v_{1f}}$ and $\vec{p_{2f}} = m_2 . \vec{v_{2f}}$ 

So for elastic collision ( not required to be head-on ) and with no net external forces we have:
    
$\text { momentum before } = \text { momentum after }$ 

$\vec{p_{1i}} + \vec{p_{2i}}  =  \vec{p_{1f}} + \vec{p_{2f}}$ 

Total vector momentum of the system of two objects colliding is conserved. It stays constant.
